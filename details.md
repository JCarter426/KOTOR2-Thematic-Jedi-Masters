### Explanation

Out of all the Masters, Zez has the worst problems. Unlike Kavar he doesn’t make any sense as a Guardian, and unlike Vrook he didn’t at least have some difficulty to make up for it. Hell, he’s not even given any Force points in his initial fight! While some might argue this is an intentional decision meant to show that Zez had abandoned the Force during his exile as the Exile herself did, that doesn’t match his usage of Force forms in the fight, if the player is of a class to learn Force forms: bottom-line, Obsidian just dropped the ball. The fight isn’t just unchallenging, it’s a downright insult.

In restructuring Zez, I took heavy inspiration from his behavior on Nar Shaddaa and his outlook on life, the Force and the Jedi as he describes it to the player. He expresses distaste with the Jedi and disaffection with the Force on a level utterly unlike Kavar or Vrook, and this, coupled with his furtiveness, led me to conclude that, of the three, he was overwhelmingly the best fit for a Sentinel: the most ‘human’ of the Jedi, the most in-touch with the skills and realities of the world beyond the grasp of the Force.

Sentinels don’t have any truly unique features aside from their immunities, however, and I wanted Zez to stand out in a way which reflected his nature. To achieve that I granted him Sneak Attack: I judged that, as the most cautious and hidden of the Masters, one who only revealed himself when forced or met with great opportunity, Sneak Attack was very much in line with his nature. From there I granted him very utilitarian powers which I felt a master of his like would find useful: Stasis, Push and Speed to flee from enemies, and Valor to support his own abilities when forced to fight.

This is because, of the Masters, Zez has been made the overall weakest. One of the conceits of KOTOR 2, as we’ll keep returning to, is that conflict strengthens while isolation weakens. Zez is skilled in hiding, but it has atrophied his other capabilities, making him very reliant on his skills and equipment. As he comes out of his isolation at the end of Nar Shaddaa he begins to recover here, and is stronger by the time of Dantooine—but also less wise by far, as he has lost the uniqueness of his perspective when faced with the stubborn surety of Kavar and Vrook.

### Explanation

Previously Vrook was the inexplicable monster, grandpa murder who could actually pose a threat to even high-quality builds. I always felt this was unreasonable; Vrook was very old, very misguided, and compared to his compatriots his interaction with the outside world was only middling. He did help around Dantooine, but again only on the periphery, not deeply challenging himself in the process. It doesn’t make sense for Vrook to be so dangerous.

But it _does_ make sense for Kavar. Kavar was a Guardian, the most martial of the Jedi, to begin with. He visually appears to be the youngest (certainly much younger than Vrook, at least), and he’s also overwhelmingly the most active, taking a visible and dangerous role as Queen Talia’s advisor. At one point we even know he foiled an assassination attempt, though against himself or the Queen is uncertain. Either way, compared to Vrook Kavar is younger, wiser, stronger, more active and more martial by temperament. Move over grandpa murder, uncle murder is here.

Kavar is bluntly a monster. He can dish out a lot of damage, but more importantly he can sustain a massive amount of punishment between his CON and Toughness feats. As the Master who is visibly the most considerate of the Exile and most apologetic about what happened to them, both on Onderon and Dantooine, he has also been granted significant WIS, enough to resist the Force abilities of a Jedi who hasn’t focused themselves into their Force potential.
Kavar is beatable, but very difficult to take down, both for dedicated melee and caster builds. He is likely more of a threat than any enemy in the game except for Sion (if using my Thematic Sith Lords mod).

### Explanation

As I discussed with Kavar, it never made sense to me for Vrook to be a Guardian. Vrook was shown to be someone who, if cantankerous, the other Masters of the training academy respected; someone who was on the Council and considered wise in the Force. Given that part of the goal of Thematic Jedi Masters is to differentiate the fights between the Masters, it made the most sense to move Vrook to being a Consular.

Now, Vrook is not exactly a pushover. He has lost much of his attribute strength, but he recovers some of it in the form of items (to represent his age, and relying on equipment to compensate more over time). Additionally he has a wide breadth of feats despite being a Consular, to represent his lifetime of experience, and a large selection of powers to assist him in combat. Vrook is still dangerous, he simply isn’t as dangerous as he used to be; he’s reliant on using his powers to strengthen himself enough to fight his foes, and is vulnerable to a strong enemy’s Force abilities due to his age-sapped attributes (and thus, weakened saving throws).

### Explanation

Atris was difficult for me to decide on. Pushover fights aren’t fun, but at the same time thematically Atris’s fight SHOULD be easy: she’s a pathetic person who’s hidden herself away, systematically lying to herself about who and what she is and sacrificing pawns to avoid having to take any action herself. Even compared to Zez-Kai Ell Atris is more reclusive, has challenged herself less. She should easily be the weakest of all the Jedi. But so weak that her fight is trivial? That’s, again, not fun.

What I hit upon is, I think, a reasonable compromise. Atris herself is tremendously weak, with very poor attributes and feats. She does command a vast array of Force powers, but almost none expertly, all being mere snippets of knowledge she’s been able to snatch away from the holocrons and pretend she’s master of. But she’s also not quite so weak as she appears: I reason that fighting amongst the holocrons does strengthen her, which I represent with Will and Reflex save bonuses, and as the guardian of the remnant of the Jedi relics I view it likely that she would use much of the artefacts at her disposal to supplement her weak natural abilities, making her more capable than her statline might initially suggest.

Even so, this fight should not be difficult, and is not meant to be. It will be very slightly more challenging than vanilla, but even with all these compensating factors Atris is still very weak, as she should be. Hiding away as she has, there was never any real prospect of her resisting the Exile.
